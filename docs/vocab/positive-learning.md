# Positive Learning

Positive learning, as defined by project management researcher Bent Flyvbjerg,
is the process of improving performance by repeating similar work through
standardized, replicable modules. Organizations that embrace positive learning
get faster, better, and cheaper at delivery over time. 

## Why It Matters for EKG

The [EKG Method](../index.md) emphasizes positive learning through:

- **Modular [use cases](../concept/use-case/index.md)** - Reusable patterns
  that can be applied across the enterprise, allowing teams to build on
  proven approaches rather than starting from scratch.
- **Iterative delivery** - Ship, learn, and improve cycles that create
  feedback loops between the organization and its environment.
- **Standardized components** - [Ontologies](../concept/ontology.md),
  [data products](../concept/data-product.md), and [stories](../concept/story.md)
  that accumulate organizational knowledge.

## Contrast with Negative Learning

Negative learning occurs when organizations treat each initiative as unique,
preventing the accumulation of expertise and causing teams to repeat the
same mistakes. This often happens in traditional "big bang" projects where:

- Each system is built as a bespoke solution
- Knowledge stays siloed within project teams
- Lessons learned are documented but never applied
- The organization doesn't get better at delivery over time

## Connection to Perpetual Learning Machine

The concept of positive learning is foundational to the
[Perpetual Learning Machine](perpetual-learning-machine.md) architectural
pattern, which provides the technical infrastructure to capture and
systematize learnings across the enterprise.
