# Outcome Stereotype

## Overview

An **Outcome Stereotype** is a categorization of an
[Outcome](index.md) based on its nature or purpose. While all outcomes
represent the "why" behind business requirements, stereotypes provide
additional semantic meaning to help organize and communicate outcomes
more effectively.

If you want to call an outcome a "Goal", "Objective", or "Definition
of Done", that's just a stereotype of Outcome.

## Common Stereotypes

The Use Case Tree Method recognizes several common outcome
stereotypes:

### Goal

A high-level, strategic outcome that provides direction and purpose.
Goals are typically broad and aspirational, guiding the overall
direction of business initiatives.

**Example:**

> "Become the leading provider of sustainable enterprise solutions in
> our market."

### Objective

A specific, measurable outcome that is more focused than a goal.
Objectives are concrete and time-bound, making progress toward goals
tangible and actionable.

**Example:**

> "Increase market share in the enterprise segment by 15% within 12
> months."

### Key Result

A quantifiable outcome that supports a goal or objective. Key Results
are the measurable indicators that show whether you're achieving your
objectives (often used in OKR frameworks).

**Example:**

> "Achieve 10,000 active enterprise customers by Q4."

### KPI (Key Performance Indicator)

A measurable outcome used to evaluate the success of an organization,
project, or activity in meeting objectives. KPIs are ongoing
measurements that track performance over time.

**Example:**

> "Maintain customer satisfaction score above 4.5/5.0."

### Success Criteria

Measurable conditions that indicate success for a particular
initiative, project, or use case. Success criteria define what "done"
looks like.

**Example:**

> "System processes 10,000 transactions per second with 99.9% uptime."

### Definition of Done

A completion criterion for a [Use Case](../use-case.md) or
[Story](../story.md). The Definition of Done specifies when a use case
or story is considered complete and ready for acceptance.

**Example:**

> "User can complete checkout in under 3 clicks, payment is processed,
> and confirmation email is sent within 30 seconds."

## Why Use Stereotypes?

Outcome Stereotypes serve several important purposes:

1. **Communication** — Different stakeholders may be familiar with
   different terminology (e.g., "Goals" vs "Objectives" vs "KPIs").
   Stereotypes help bridge these communication gaps.

2. **Organization** — Stereotypes help categorize and organize
   outcomes in a hierarchy or structure that makes sense for your
   organization.

3. **Clarity** — Stereotypes can clarify the level of abstraction or
   specificity intended for an outcome.

4. **Alignment** — Stereotypes help align with existing frameworks and
   methodologies (e.g., OKRs, KPIs, Agile acceptance criteria).

## Fundamental Principle

Despite these different stereotypes, the Use Case Tree Method
maintains a fundamental principle:

!!! principle "Outcome is the Base Concept" All of these stereotypes
are fundamentally **Outcomes**. They all represent the "why" behind
business requirements---the desired results, benefits, or states we
want to achieve.

The method uses "Outcome" as the base concept, with stereotypes
providing additional semantic meaning only when needed. This approach:

- Simplifies the core model
- Reduces unnecessary complexity
- Allows flexibility in adopting existing organizational terminology
- Maintains focus on the essential purpose: expressing desired results

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### Use Cases

[Use Cases](../use-case.md) are linked to Outcomes through their
desired results. Different outcome stereotypes may be appropriate at
different levels:

- **Goals** may guide entire programs or product lines
- **Objectives** may drive specific use case implementations
- **Success Criteria** and **Definitions of Done** may define use case
  completion

### Stories

[Stories](../story.md) deliver outcomes through specific scenarios.
The outcome stereotype helps clarify what success looks like for the
story:

- **Definition of Done** — When is this story complete?
- **Success Criteria** — How do we measure story success?
- **Key Results** — What measurable impact should this story have?

### Business Capabilities

Business Capabilities (as modeled in the [Use Case Tree](../use-case-tree.md)) enable outcomes.
Higher-level outcome stereotypes (Goals, Objectives) often drive
capability investment decisions, while lower-level stereotypes (KPIs,
Success Criteria) measure capability performance.

## Best Practices

1. **Choose stereotypes that resonate** — Use terminology that your
   organization and stakeholders already understand and use.

2. **Don't over-categorize** — Not every outcome needs a stereotype.
   Use them when they add clarity, not complexity.

3. **Maintain consistency** — If you use a stereotype like "Goal" for
   strategic outcomes, use it consistently across your documentation.

4. **Focus on the outcome, not the label** — The stereotype is less
   important than clearly expressing the desired result.

5. **Allow evolution** — Your understanding of outcome stereotypes may
   evolve as your organization's needs change.
